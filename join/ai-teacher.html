AI Teacher

Կրթություն Education

Don’t just teach AI. Show people what they can do with it.

We are looking for an AI enthusiast, hands-on practitioner, and natural educator who is excited about making AI useful for everyone.

At AI Qez Ban, teaching AI isn’t about explaining machine learning algorithms or overwhelming people with technical theory. It’s about helping people discover how AI can change the way they work, solve problems, create things, and approach challenges they’ve been dealing with for years.

We want someone who doesn’t just talk about AI but actively uses it. Someone who experiments with new tools, builds practical solutions, discovers better workflows, and gets genuinely excited when a tool makes something possible that wasn’t possible before.

Your job is to turn that excitement into practical skills that other people can use.

You might teach a marketer how to build an AI-powered content workflow, help a project manager automate repetitive tasks, show a business owner how to work with AI agents, or help someone with no technical background discover what they can accomplish with the right tools.

You don’t need to be a machine learning engineer. You need to understand AI deeply enough to use it effectively, explain it clearly, and help others do the same.

Who We Are

AI Qez Ban is building a community of people who want to understand AI, use it in their everyday work, and discover what it makes possible.

Our mission is ambitious: one million AI-literate Armenians within five years.

We believe AI literacy should go beyond knowing which tools exist. People should know how to choose the right tool, work with it effectively, evaluate its output, build useful workflows, and apply it to real problems.

That’s why we focus on practical, hands-on AI education for professionals, teams, and organizations across different fields.

We’re building an environment where people learn by doing, experiment without fear, share what works, and continuously improve how they use AI. And we want our teachers to embody exactly that mindset.

The Role in One Line

You make AI understandable, practical, and useful for people across professions — through engaging teaching, real-world examples, and hands-on learning experiences.

You’ll research and experiment with AI tools, design practical learning experiences, deliver interactive sessions, and help learners build the confidence to apply what they’ve learned in their own work.

You won’t be expected to know every AI tool or have an answer to every question. You will be expected to stay curious, test things yourself, understand what you’re teaching, and help learners figure things out rather than simply showing them what to click.

Key Responsibilities

1. AI Exploration & Practical Expertise

AI changes quickly. We need teachers who actively explore that change instead of relying on what they learned six months ago.

  • Regularly explore, test, and evaluate AI tools, models, features, and workflows, including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, NotebookLM, Gamma, Grok, AI agents, and emerging technologies.
  • Go beyond surface-level experimentation. Understand what tools can actually do, where they perform well, where they fail, and when they’re worth using.
  • Apply AI to real tasks in your own work: research, analysis, writing, presentations, content creation, problem-solving, automation, and other relevant workflows.
  • Experiment with advanced capabilities such as custom instructions, projects, knowledge sources, multimodal inputs, integrations, and agentic workflows where relevant.
  • Identify practical use cases that can help people become more productive, creative, informed, and effective in their work.
  • Compare tools and approaches, understanding their strengths, limitations, costs, and practical trade-offs.
  • Turn your discoveries into useful demonstrations, exercises, examples, and learning materials.

The standard: You teach from experience, not from a list of features copied from a tool’s website.

2. Teaching & Live Session Delivery

Knowing how to use AI and knowing how to teach it are two different skills. We need someone who can do both.

  • Deliver engaging, interactive workshops and lessons in online, offline, and hybrid formats.
  • Explain AI concepts, tools, and workflows in simple, accessible language without unnecessary jargon.
  • Demonstrate real tasks step by step, explaining not only what you’re doing but why you’re doing it.
  • Encourage learners to experiment during sessions rather than passively watch demonstrations.
  • Guide participants through exercises, troubleshoot problems, answer questions, and help them get unstuck.
  • Adapt your explanations and teaching pace to different skill levels, professions, and learning needs.
  • Use relatable examples from everyday work so learners can immediately understand the relevance of what they’re learning.
  • Create an environment where people feel comfortable asking questions, making mistakes, and trying unfamiliar things.
  • Help learners distinguish between impressive AI demonstrations and solutions that are genuinely useful in practice.

The standard: Learners shouldn’t leave saying, “That was an interesting presentation.” They should leave saying, “I understand how this works, and I know what I’m going to use it for.”

3. Learning Experience & Content Development

Great AI education doesn’t happen by opening ChatGPT and improvising for two hours. It requires thoughtful learning design, relevant examples, and exercises that lead to meaningful outcomes.

  • Design hands-on sessions around real problems, practical outcomes, and clearly defined learning objectives.
  • Develop demonstrations, exercises, prompts, templates, workflows, and supporting materials that learners can reuse after the session.
  • Structure lessons so that learners progress from understanding a concept to applying it independently.
  • Create examples relevant to different professional contexts, including marketing, management, operations, education, content creation, research, and business.
  • Collaborate with the education team and domain experts to identify meaningful use cases and develop relevant learning experiences.
  • Adapt materials based on the audience, their existing knowledge, and the challenges they want to solve.
  • Review and update learning content as tools evolve, interfaces change, and better approaches emerge.
  • Identify common learner difficulties and improve explanations, exercises, and examples to address them.

The standard: Every session should have a clear purpose and a practical outcome. Learners should gain something they can apply beyond the classroom.

What We Need From You

  • Genuine AI proficiency. You regularly use AI tools in your own work or personal projects. You understand how to get useful results, recognize limitations, and move beyond basic prompts into practical workflows.
  • A builder’s mindset. You enjoy experimenting, solving problems, connecting tools, and figuring out how things work. When you discover an interesting capability, your first instinct is to try it yourself.
  • Clear communication. You can explain complex ideas simply, present confidently, demonstrate workflows, and keep people engaged without hiding behind technical jargon.
  • Practical problem-solving. You can look at a real task and identify where AI could help, which approach makes sense, and how to evaluate whether the solution actually works.
  • A learner-first attitude. You care about what participants understand and achieve, not just how much material you cover. You can adapt your teaching instead of expecting everyone to learn the same way.
  • Initiative and curiosity. You don’t wait for someone to tell you which tool to explore or which use case to investigate. You actively discover opportunities and bring ideas to the team.
  • Continuous learning. You’re comfortable with a fast-changing field where yesterday’s best approach may no longer be today’s best approach. You learn quickly, accept feedback, and enjoy improving your craft.
  • Collaboration. You can work with fellow teachers, instructional designers, and domain experts to create stronger learning experiences together.
  • Mission alignment. You believe practical AI literacy can create meaningful opportunities for people and organizations in Armenia, and you want to contribute to that change.

Experience & Background

  • At least 2 years of professional experience in any field where you have developed practical skills, solved problems, or built expertise that you can bring into your teaching.
  • Strong practical familiarity with AI tools and their application to real-world tasks.
  • Strong communication and presentation skills.
  • Previous teaching, workshop facilitation, mentoring, or public speaking experience is a significant plus.
  • Experience in a specific professional domain — such as marketing, business operations, project management, education, design, content creation, or automation — is valuable because it gives you real problems and workflows to teach from.

You don’t need a traditional teaching background, a data science degree, or a software engineering career. What matters is what you can do with AI, how well you understand it, and how effectively you can help someone else learn.

What We Offer

  • The opportunity to help shape practical AI education in Armenia.
  • A platform to share your expertise and turn your experiments into learning experiences that reach diverse audiences.
  • An environment where curiosity, experimentation, and new ideas are encouraged.
  • Collaboration with people who are passionate about AI, education, and building useful things.
  • The opportunity to continuously develop your AI expertise, teaching skills, and professional network.
  • The chance to contribute to a mission that goes beyond individual workshops: making AI literacy accessible to a million Armenians.
