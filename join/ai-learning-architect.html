About AI Qez Ban

AI Qez Ban is building a community of AI-literate people and helping organizations across Armenia turn AI into practical results.

Our mission is ambitious: 1M+ AI-literate Armenians within five years, and Armenia as a global exporter of AI transformation by 2030.

We believe AI education should go beyond learning which tools exist or how to write better prompts. People need to understand how AI can help them solve real problems, rethink their workflows, work more effectively, and discover entirely new possibilities.

That’s why we build practical, hands-on AI learning experiences for individuals, professionals, teams, and organizations across different industries.

Our approach is learner-first, problem-driven, and AI-first. We experiment, challenge traditional approaches to education, and continuously improve how people learn and apply AI in the real world.

About the Role

Every program is different. A course for lawyers is nothing like a course for marketers, and neither one looks like a course for doctors. You learn how these people actually work, identify where AI can genuinely help them, and build a program around their needs.

Then you make it happen: the research, the curriculum, the teachers, the sessions, and the program itself while it runs. If it works, that is your win. If it does not, that is yours too.

You don’t need to be an AI engineer or researcher. You need to be an AI enthusiast: someone who uses these tools every day, tries new ones as they come out, and gets excited about what people can do with them.

Work arrangement: flexible and responsibility-based. We expect you to be there for client meetings, program launches, and key offline sessions. The rest is up to you: you choose where and when you work, based on what serves the goals of the role best.

What You Will Do

You own a program from the first client conversation to the final result.

Understand the Client

  • Meet the client and learn how their organization really works: the teams, the roles, and the daily workflows.
  • Find out who your learners are and where their real problems and opportunities lie.
  • Run digital and AI literacy assessments to understand the group’s actual starting level.
  • Turn all of this into a clear answer to one question: what does this program need to achieve?

Research the Field

  • Go deep into the client’s profession. Learn how lawyers, doctors, marketers, civil servants, or other professionals actually work.
  • Test which AI tools and use cases genuinely help in that field, and which ones only look good on a slide.
  • Bring in domain experts and interview them when you need expertise we don’t have in-house.
  • Validate your use cases with those experts, so what we teach holds up in front of a room of professionals.

Design the Program

  • Build the curriculum: the program structure, sessions, sequence, learning outcomes, and practical use cases at the heart of each session.
  • Design how the program teaches, not just what it covers. We learn by doing and solving real problems.
  • Create pre- and post-assessments, quizzes, and surveys that show whether the program actually worked.
  • Prepare the materials, frameworks, and structure that teachers will build on.

Work with the Teachers

  • Choose the right teacher for each session from our teacher pool.
  • Share the program’s vision and brief teachers on their sessions: the audience, goals, use cases, skill level, and tone.
  • Shape sessions together. Sometimes you review what teachers build; often you build it with them.
  • Keep the program coherent, so that ten sessions by ten teachers feel like one learning journey.

Own the Delivery and the Result

  • Be there while the program runs. Observe sessions, listen to learners, gather feedback, and fix things while there is still time.
  • Stay in touch with the client so problems surface early and expectations remain aligned.
  • Work closely with the Learning Experience Coordinator, who handles the operational and technical side of delivery.
  • Measure results and report honestly on what worked and what didn’t.
  • Turn every program into something reusable: better methods, better use cases, better assessments, and AI-powered systems that make the next program faster and stronger.
